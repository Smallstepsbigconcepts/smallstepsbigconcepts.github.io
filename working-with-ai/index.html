Systems

What working with an AI assistant actually looks like

No magic. It's closer to having a sharp new crew member who never forgets the checklist.

The real setup

How it works

  • Every project is a maintenance card, like on the boat: what it is, what “done” means, where it stands, the next three actions with an owner, and what repeats. 30 active cards right now.
  • A small crew, each with one job: a general manager (picks the day's ONE thing), a seller, a clip poster, and a builder for SSBC products.
  • The 80% rule: anything at 80% or more gets reported every week as shipped, blocked, or shelved. Nothing quietly dies.
  • I approve, it drafts. No post, price change, purchase, or deletion happens without my OK.
  • Numbers come from real sources. Guesses get labeled. Old versions get saved before anything changes.
Behind the scenes

Wins you don't see

  • 844 Evernote notes exported into plain files I own (Oct 9, 2026).
  • My PC scanned and mapped: about 72,000 files, 902 duplicate copies. The folder move waits until after taxes.
  • Getting caught up on three years of back taxes, with the paperwork organized into one packet.
  • Stories captured through Q&A interviews in my own voice, instead of me staring at a blank page.
Lessons learned

Fewer moving parts

  • An earlier setup ran about 42 automated jobs and got tangled. The current plan has about 15.
  • Automated doesn't mean resolved.
What it doesn't do

It's not the boss

  • It doesn't decide for me.
  • It doesn't post as me without approval.
  • It's sometimes confidently wrong, so I check anything that matters.
